Most written histories of the Bemba begin with Abena Ng'andu, the Crocodile clan, from whom every Chitimukulu is chosen. That story is real and important. But it is the history of a kingship, not of every clan.

My research asks a different question: which clans were here first? The tradition I am documenting holds that the oldest clan is **Abena Mfula**, the Rain clan, followed by **Abena Mumba**, the Clay clan, and then **Abena Miti**, the Trees.

## The order has its own logic

Rain falls first. The earth receives it and holds it. From rain and earth, trees grow. Read this way, the order of the clans is also an order in nature, a lesson about dependency rather than rivalry.

## Seniority is not the same as power

The kingship of Chitimukulu belongs to the Crocodile clan, which arrived with a migrating royal line. That is political primacy. Seniority is something else: the standing of the clans who were already on the land, often as custodians of rain, land and burial rites. Many African states grew by absorbing older "owners of the land", who kept ritual roles long after new rulers arrived.

## Many peoples, one clan system

This research reaches beyond the Bemba of Chitimukulu. Abena Mumba are closely associated with the Ushi. The same clans run through the Chishinga, Kabende, Ng'umbo, Mukulu and Unga, the Lunda of Mwata Kazembe, the Bisa, the Lala, the Tabwa and the Lungu. Each people has its own history, and none is a footnote to another.

## How you can help

If you are an elder, or have elders who would speak about this, these are the questions I am asking:

1. Which clan is the oldest, and how do you know?
2. Which comes second, and third?
3. Is there a story that explains why Abena Mfula came first?
4. Which clans were on the land when the Abena Ng'andu arrived?
5. Have you heard a different order? What was it?

Different accounts are welcome. Where elders disagree, that disagreement is part of the history too. Every contribution will be recorded with consent and credited as the contributor wishes. See the [Research page]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}) for more.
