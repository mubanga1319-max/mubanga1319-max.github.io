Ask a young person in Lusaka or Kitwe for their surname and they will answer at once. Ask for their clan and many will pause. Some will guess. Some will say they have never been told.

That pause matters. Among the Bemba-speaking peoples, the clan, *umukowa*, is not a decoration on the family name. It tells you who your people are, whom you may marry, who carries duties at your wedding and your funeral, and who will recognise you as kin in a town where you know nobody.

## Three words that hold a family together

Bemba kinship rests on three words. *Umukowa* (plural *imikowa*) is the clan you inherit. *Ulupwa* is the wider family you belong to and are responsible for. *Intambi* are the customs through which that knowledge is lived: naming, marriage, funerals, the way we greet one another.

In the village, children absorbed all of this without effort. In town, the household shrinks to parents and children, Town Bemba mixes with English, and the chain quietly breaks.

## Your clan comes through your mother

The Bemba are matrilineal. Your clan is your mother's clan, which was her mother's, and her mother's before that. Your father's clan still matters: you are known as a child of his people, and his relatives have duties towards you. But you do not become a member of his clan.

> Your clan is your mother's mother's clan. Your father's clan is his mother's clan.

## Draw your four-grandparent map

The simplest tool I know is a map with you in the centre and your four grandparents around you. Write each grandparent's clan. The one below you, your mother's mother, is your own clan. The one above, your father's mother, is your father's clan. The other two show you where else you have kin.

Most people can fill in two boxes on the first try. Filling in all four usually takes one good conversation with an elder.

## Questions to ask this week

- Which clan are we, and what does our totem mean?
- Which clans are our joking partners, and why?
- Who was I named after, and what kind of person were they?
- Which village and chief did our family belong to?

Ask in Bemba where you can, even imperfectly. Bring a small gift. Ask permission to record. Write everything down the same day.

## Why it matters now

Knowing your clan does not divide you from other Zambians. It does the opposite. The same clans appear among peoples who speak different languages. A Mwina Mfula from Mansa and a Mwina Mfula from Mongu can recognise one another as kin without sharing a first language. In a country where politics too often follows provincial lines, that is a connection worth keeping.

So start with one question to your mother or your grandmother: *Ndi mwina nani?*
