About

Mubanga Mwansa

Zambian technologist, researcher and mindset strategist, exploring where psychology, philosophy and African history meet.

I am the founder and managing director of Insansu Venture, an agribusiness and food-processing company in Zambia. Alongside that work, I research African indigenous knowledge systems: our languages, our clans and totems, our traditional leadership, and the ecological wisdom carried in our ceremonies.

I write as a native Bemba speaker and a member of the Mumba clan, from inside the knowledge I describe. My aim is that Bantu languages and the institutions they carry should be studied as knowledge systems, with the same seriousness we give to any other science, and that young Zambians should be able to find their way back to them.

This site is the written home of Ilyashi pa Nsaka: essays, research in progress, and practical guides.

Current work

  • A research paper on the Bemba quantifier -onse, and a research programme on Bantu languages as knowledge systems
  • Fieldwork on clan seniority across the Bemba-speaking peoples
  • A policy proposal for an optional clan field on the National Registration Card
  • Our Names, Our Kinship, a public programme on clan names for young people

Why Ilyashi pa Nsaka

Ilyashi pa nsaka means the conversation at the nsaka, the shelter at the heart of the village where knowledge was shared and passed on. In the past, the nsaka was mostly a place for men. This project keeps its spirit and opens its doors.

Here, everyone has a seat. The knowledge of mothers and grandmothers, who carry the clan in a matrilineal society, stands beside the knowledge of fathers and elders. Young people growing up in town are as welcome as those raised in the village. And every people and clan has a voice: the Bemba of Chitimukulu, the Ushi, the Luapula peoples, the Bisa, the Lala, the Tabwa, the Lungu, and all Zambians who come to listen and to share.
